My candidacy for master's programs in public policy, 2027 entry

I am positioning myself as an economist who connects evidence with public decisions. My question is how population change forces policy to change: Colombia is ageing, Venezuelan migration has transformed its labour market, and more than half of its workers are informal. Pensions, social protection and employment policy therefore have to be designed for the country of twenty years from now, and for how people will respond to each rule. I want to prepare to lead the institutions that make those decisions.

Policies are designed for a population that moves: it ages, it migrates, and it responds to incentives. I combine demography and public choice to help institutions anticipate that movement and decide well.

My profile brings together three fields: demography, which shows how a population changes; public choice, which explains how governments, voters and interest groups decide, and with what incentives; and public policy, where both become decisions. I apply them at three scales:

Country

Demographic transition

Colombia is ageing, Venezuelan migration has reshaped the labour market, and the demographic dividend is closing. More than half of employed workers are informal (54.5%, DANE, July to September 2026).

Family

Shocks and protection

The same loss can leave one household protected and another exposed, depending on income, earlier decisions and access to social protection.

Decision

Incentives and behaviour

A policy works through the incentives it creates. That is why I focus on mechanism design and behavioural economics, not only on measurement.

What I still need, and why an MPP

I have the technical training. What I have had to improvise is what happens after the analysis: the political negotiation, the behavioural responses and the public finance decisions that determine whether a policy is implemented.

Another economics degree would sharpen what I already do well; the MPP would build what I have had to improvise.

Why now

Colombia's demographic window is closing while mine is opening: I have moved from producing analysis to leading a strategy and taking part in institutional decisions.

Where I am going

Within five years, a lead poverty and equity economist at a multilateral institution such as the World Bank. Longer term, one of three paths with the same thread, population and public decisions: directing DANE; leading the demography, public choice and public policy agenda at the OECD, the World Bank or the IDB; or leading a portfolio at UNDP's Human Development Report Office.

Three questions a committee may ask, which a letter answers best

In my one-on-one session with Princeton admissions, three risks in my profile came up. A letter addresses them with more authority than my own essays, because it comes from someone who has seen my work.

1

"Is he too technical?"

An example of leadership helps more than a description of methods: a team coordinated, a conversation with a government, a decision that changed because of the work.

2

"Why not another economics degree?"

A sentence on why my next step is about leadership rather than specialisation carries real weight when it comes from someone else.

3

"How much real experience does he have?"

Besides 10 professional years, I have 4 before graduating: Divergencia, research assistance and teaching assistance, followed by lecturing. Those who knew me then can speak to it.

What makes a letter carry weight with a committee

The relationship. Since when and in what role you know me, and how closely we have worked together.
The comparison. Where you would place me among others you have supervised or taught, with a percentage or a specific group if possible.
Two examples. The situation, what I did and what changed. One example of leadership is worth more than three adjectives.
Why this program, now. Why my next step is about leadership and public decisions, and what I would bring to a cohort of experienced professionals.
The recommendation. A clear closing sentence.

One to two pages, on letterhead if possible. One base letter works for every program; only the program name and the final paragraph need adjusting.

What helps most

  • A moment when you saw me lead or persuade others.
  • The effect of my work on people or decisions, more than on indicators.
  • How I have grown over time, if you have known me for several years.
  • For the demography programs (Oxford, LSE), my capacity for research; for the policy programs (Princeton, Michigan, Cornell), my leadership.
